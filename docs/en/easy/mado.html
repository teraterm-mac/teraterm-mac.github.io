Show several sessions in one window (TTMado)

You can show several connections side by side inside one window (split panes). Just drag tabs or windows to place them left and right or top and bottom, and drag them back to make them tabs again.
This is a built-in plugin of the Mac version only, called "TTMado".

Placing panes

  • Drag a tab: drag a tab from the tab bar onto the terminal of the same window. A blue frame shows where it will go; release to place it.
  • Drop a window: drag another window by its title bar onto the terminal of the window you want. Release when the frame appears and the window moves inside. A window that is already split keeps its layout.
  • From the menu: in a window with several tabs, choose [Window] > [Stacked] or [Side By Side] to show the tabs as panes.

Where it goes depends on the mouse position:

  • Near an edge of another pane: next to that pane.
  • At the very edge of the window: along that whole side.
  • Between panes: changes their order.

At the window edges and on the tab bar, the frame also says what happens when you release (for example "Along the edge"). To cancel, release where no frame is shown or press esc.


Dragging to the right edge of the window: the pane goes along the whole right side (Along the edge)


Dragging over the lower part of the middle pane: the pane goes below it

Pane headers

Each pane has a header with the connection name. It works like a normal tab:

  • Click: selects the pane. Typing and menu commands go to the selected pane (the one with the colored frame).
  • Drag: moves the pane. Release on the tab bar at the top to make it a tab again, or outside the window to make it a window.
  • ×: appears on the left when the mouse is over the header. Click it to close that pane.
  • Right-click: shows the pane menu (below).

Drag the dividers between panes to resize them.


When the mouse is over a header, × appears on the left

Pane menu (right-click a header)

  • Split This Pane Side by Side / Top to Bottom: divides this pane again and places the next tab (or the tab you marked with ✓ by ⇧-clicking).
  • Extend to Edge: stretches this pane to the top, bottom, left or right edge (for example, two panes above and one below: stretch the upper right pane down to the bottom).
  • Back to Tab / Move to New Window: takes only this pane out.
  • Close: closes this pane's connection.

Opening a new session

In a split window, [New connection], [Duplicate session] and [Local Shell Connection] ask where to open:

  • Open in This Window: next to the selected pane.
  • Open in a Tab: in a new tab.
  • Open in New Window: in a separate window.

Number of panes

A split window shows how many panes it holds as a round number badge (for example ⑥): to the right of its tab, or at the right of the title bar when there is no tab bar.


Three on top, three at the bottom. There is a tab bar, so the number appears on the tab


Without a tab bar, the number appears at the right of the title bar

Going back

  • To return one pane, drag its header to the tab bar at the top, or right-click it and choose [Back to Tab].
  • When a pane's connection closes, the pane disappears and the rest grow. With one pane left, the split ends.
  • The window's close button closes all connections in it.

Turning it off

In [Setup] > [Additional settings...], open the [Plugins] tab, uncheck "TTMado" and click OK. It takes effect at once: split panes go back to tabs, and tabs work like normal macOS tabs. Check it again to turn it back on.
