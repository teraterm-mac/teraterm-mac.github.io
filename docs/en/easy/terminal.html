Terminal basics


A terminal window: the in-window menu bar at the top and the ≡ button at the top right

Copy and paste

  1. Drag with the mouse over the text you want. Double-click selects a word, triple-click a line.
  2. Press ⌘C to copy.
  3. Press ⌘V to paste. The text is sent to the host.

When you paste several lines at once, a confirmation dialog may appear. Check the text before sending it.

Scrolling

  • Lines that scrolled off the screen can be seen again by scrolling up with the mouse wheel or two fingers on the trackpad.
  • Typing takes you back to the bottom (the current screen).
  • To clear the screen or the saved lines, choose [Edit] > [Clear screen] or [Clear buffer].

Tabs and windows

  • A new connection (⌘N) opens as a tab in the current window.
  • Switch tabs by clicking them or with ⌘⇧[ / ⌘⇧].
  • Drag a tab out of the window to make it a separate window.
  • To open one more connection to the same host, choose [File] > [Duplicate session] (⌘D).
  • To work on this Mac itself, choose [File] > [Local Shell Connection] (⌘G). It opens the same shell as Terminal.app.
  • To arrange several windows, choose [Window] > [Stacked] or [Side By Side]. To put them back, choose the "Undo - …" item in the same menu.
  • Drag a tab onto the terminal inside the window to show several sessions side by side in one window (split panes). See Show several sessions in one window.


Two windows arranged with [Window] > [Stacked]

Show and hide the menu bar

The menu bar inside the window has the same menus as Tera Term for Windows. Click a title to open it.


The [File] menu opened from the in-window menu bar

  • Click the ≡ button at the right of the title bar to hide the menu bar. Click it again to show it.
  • You can also use "Hide Menu Bar" / "Show Menu Bar" in the [Window] menu, or right-click the menu bar to hide it.
  • Even when it is hidden, the same menus are always in the Mac menu bar at the top of the screen. Control-clicking the terminal also shows them.
